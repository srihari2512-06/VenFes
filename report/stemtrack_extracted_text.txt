ABSTRACT
Human stem cell research, regenerative medicine, and somatic cell reprogramming
(e.g., induced pluripotent stem cells, iPSCs) demand uncompromising standards of
biological lineage provenance and institutional ethical compliance. Standard Labora-
tory Information Management Systems (LIMS) and relational registries treat biologi-
cal inventory as static tabular database records with in-place updates. This paradigm
catastrophically overwrites historical cellular states, obliterates derivation audit trails
(such as passages P0 to P1, split colonies, and freeze-thaw cycles), and fails to prop-
agate upstream donor consent withdrawals down to derived daughter cultures.
To resolve these critical bio-informatics and regulatory vulnerabilities, this project
presents StemTrack: An Event-Sourced Biological Lineage and Stem Cell
Ethical Governance Platform. Engineered upon the full-stack MERN (MongoDB,
Express.js, React 18, Node.js) technology stack, StemTrack models biological speci-
mens not as isolated database records, but as an event-sourced Directed Acyclic
Graph (DAG) tracking cellular evolution:
Donor −→Consent −→Intake Tissue Sample −→Primary Culture (P0) −→
Passages (P1, P2...) −→CryoVials / Experiments
The core technical architecture introduces three mathematical and regulatory in-
variants:
(i) DAG Cycle Guard: Powered by a Breadth-First Search (BFS) reachability
traversal (O(V + E) complexity) that mathematically proves no cell derivation
can introduce circular causality or self-referential parent-child loops;
(ii) Institutional Compliance Gate: An automated verification layer that asserts
active Institutional Review Board (IRB) ethical approval protocols and unre-
voked donor consent before permitting derivations, while automatically tagging
downstream derivatives upon upstream consent withdrawal;
(iii) Atomic Multi-Document ACID Transactions: Multi-document MongoDB
sessions that guarantee parent status updates, child node derivations, directed
lineage edges, and immutable audit logs succeed or fail atomically.
Furthermore, StemTrack features an interactive 10×10 cryogenic slot bio-repository
visualizer, 3-tier Role-Based Access Control (admin, researcher, donor), real-time
KPI dashboards, and RFC 4180 CSV export capability.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1
OVERVIEW
Stem cell bio-banking, regenerative therapeutics, and cellular engineering represent
one of the most promising yet technically complex frontiers in modern clinical medicine.
Biological specimens in stem cell laboratories do not remain static; they evolve over
time through multiple derivation stages: primary tissue isolation, enzymatic dissoci-
ation, feeder layer plating, sequential cellular passaging (P0 →P1 →P2), colony
splitting, cryogenic freezing in liquid nitrogen bio-repositories (−196◦C), and con-
trolled revival for downstream experimental differentiation assays.
Throughout this multi-generational lifecycle, laboratories must maintain complete
lineage traceability while complying with strict legal frameworks governing human bi-
ological specimens, such as Institutional Review Board (IRB) ethical protocols and
informed donor consent mandates. When a donor exercises the legal right to revoke
or withdraw biological consent, every cell line, cryogenic vial, and downstream dif-
ferentiation assay derived from that donor must be flagged and isolated immediately
across the laboratory.
StemTrack is designed to address this challenge by modeling biological materials
as an event-sourced Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). Developed using the MERN
stack (MongoDB, Express, React, Node.js), StemTrack combines mathematical graph
validation with automated compliance checks to ensure biological integrity and ethical
governance across all research workflows.
1.2
PROBLEM DEFINITION
Traditional Laboratory Information Management Systems (LIMS) and relational bio-
logical registries exhibit fundamental architectural deficiencies when applied to stem
cell research:
• Destructive State Mutation: Relational registries rely on in-place SQL UPDATE
queries (e.g., modifying a passage number from 0 to 1 directly), erasing earlier
phenotypic states and making it impossible to audit cellular evolution over time.
• Lack of Branching Provenance: Standard relational databases model parent-
child relationships through foreign keys and join tables.
These structures are

inefficient for traversing multi-generational cellular lineage trees and lack formal
graph invariant validation to prevent invalid cyclical edges.
• Disconnected Ethical Governance: Donor consent status and IRB protocols
are typically tracked in external spreadsheets or physical paper binders.
If a
donor withdraws consent, laboratory scientists receive no automated notifications,
risking the illegal utilization of revoked specimens in downstream assays.
• Physical Storage Disconnect: Cryogenic inventory systems track freezer rack
positions but fail to correlate physical coordinates with biological passage numbers,
morphological viability, and ethical compliance status.
• Vulnerability to Concurrency Anomalies: When multiple researchers pas-
sage or split cell cultures concurrently, non-transactional database updates result
in orphan lineage nodes, duplicated passage numbers, and phantom freezer slot
assignments.
1.3
OBJECTIVE
The primary objective of this project is to architect, develop, and validate StemTrack
as an enterprise-grade biological lineage tracking and stem cell ethical governance
platform. Specific technical objectives include:
1. Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) Modeling: Formulate an event-sourced data
model representing biological specimens, cellular passages, and split derivations as
a directed acyclic graph.
2. Mathematical Cycle Guard: Implement an in-memory Breadth-First Search
(BFS) reachability algorithm that validates graph acyclicity before any derivation
edge is persisted (O(V + E) complexity).
3. Automated Compliance Gate: Construct middleware and controller-level se-
curity checks that assert active donor consent and valid IRB approval prior to
permitting biological derivations.
4. ACID Transaction Management: Orchestrate multi-document MongoDB trans-
actions to guarantee atomic execution of passage increments, split colony deriva-
tions, edge creation, and audit logging.
5. Cryogenic Bio-Repository Mapping: Provide a visual 100-slot (10×10) cryo-
genic rack management interface with real-time occupancy indicators and slot
collision guards.

1.4
SCOPE OF THE PROJECT
The scope of StemTrack encompasses stem cell research institutes, regenerative medicine
biobanks, university biology laboratories, and clinical trial centers.
The platform
manages the complete biological lifecycle from initial donor intake to experimental
assay consumption. It provides granular 3-tier Role-Based Access Control (admin,
researcher, donor), cryptographic password hashing, JWT bearer token security,
RFC 4180 audit trail reporting, and real-time analytical metrics.
1.5
MOTIVATION
The primary motivation behind StemTrack stems from the high regulatory risks and
scientific irreproducibility challenges confronting modern stem cell laboratories. The
International Society for Stem Cell Research (ISSCR) guidelines and federal regula-
tions mandate strict provenance tracking and immediate enforcement of donor consent
withdrawals. Inadvertently using cells from a revoked donor in published clinical tri-
als invalidates years of research and incurs severe institutional penalties. By fusing
event-sourced graph structures with strict compliance gates, StemTrack establishes a
dependable, automated foundation for ethical regenerative medicine.

CHAPTER 2
RELATED WORK
2.1
LITERATURE REVIEW
Managing biological specimens and their derivations has evolved across three primary
generations of laboratory software:
1. Relational Laboratory Information Management Systems (LIMS): Sys-
tems like LabWare and OpenSpecimen rely on relational databases (SQL). While
effective for standard hospital sample tracking and billing, their tabular schemas
struggle to represent recursive branching, requiring complex recursive Common
Table Expressions (CTEs) that degrade under deep generational hierarchies.
2. Bio-Banking Inventory Registries: Commercial biobanks often prioritize phys-
ical inventory (freezer, shelf, rack, box) using barcode scanners. However, these
systems treat biological entities as static objects rather than evolving organisms,
omitting cellular derivations, split colonies, and passage numbers.
3. Event Sourcing and Directed Acyclic Graphs in Bioinformatics: Recent
literature highlights the advantages of event sourcing and DAG structures for
modeling genomic workflows and molecular genealogies. Rather than mutating
state in place, event sourcing records every state change as an immutable event.
StemTrack applies this concept directly to cell culture operations, pairing it with
graph visualization.
4. Ethical Governance Frameworks: Studies on bioethics emphasize the need
for dynamic consent models where donor preferences can change over time. Auto-
mated governance systems must enforce these changes upstream to prevent non-
compliant research before materials are thawed or plated.
5. Graph Traversal and Cycle Prevention in Workflow Engines: Directed
Acyclic Graphs are standard in build systems (Make, Bazel) and computational
biology pipelines (Nextflow, Snakemake). Applying cycle prevention algorithms
(BFS/DFS) ensures that cellular derivations cannot generate self-referential bio-
logical paradoxes.
6. ACID Transactions in Modern Document Databases: MongoDB’s intro-
duction of multi-document ACID transactions allows NoSQL systems to handle
4


=== PAGE 14 ===
complex multi-entity mutations reliably, bridging the gap between flexible JSON
schemas and transactional safety.
7. Interactive Lineage Visualization in Modern Web UIs: Advances in web-
based graph rendering (React Flow, D3.js) enable responsive visualization of large
cellular lineage networks directly within browser dashboards.
8. Cryogenic Asset Management Standards: International biobanking stan-
dards require accurate temperature, rack coordinate, and vial-level tracking to
prevent specimen degradation and inventory loss.
2.2
EXISTING SYSTEM
Conventional stem cell laboratory infrastructure relies on a mixture of fragmented
systems:
• Paper laboratory notebooks and physical binder logs recording passage dates and
split ratios.
• Relational SQL database tables with flat foreign keys (parent_id) that lack cyclic
invariant validation.
• Manual email or spreadsheet notifications regarding donor consent withdrawals,
often resulting in human communication failures.
• Cryogenic freezer map spreadsheets that are manually updated, leading to dupli-
cate slot allocations and misplaced specimens.
2.2.1
Drawbacks of the Existing System
• Data Inconsistency: Destructive updates overwrite prior passage numbers, elim-
inating historical traceability.
• Regulatory Vulnerability: Lack of automated consent gating allows researchers
to accidentally utilize revoked tissue samples.
• High Latency in Lineage Audits: Determining the full ancestral lineage of a
cell line requires hours of manual database queries and cross-referencing.
• Slot Collisions: Multiple researchers claiming the same cryogenic rack position
causes physical storage confusion and wasted laboratory reagents.

2.3
PROPOSED SYSTEM
StemTrack replaces legacy registries with an event-sourced, DAG-native architecture:
• Immutable Graph Derivations: Every passage, split, cryopreservation, and
retrieval is modeled as an immutable node and edge within a Directed Acyclic
Graph.
• Automated Compliance Gate: Upstream API middleware verifies donor con-
sent and IRB approval status before allowing any derivation or consumption.
• Mathematical Cycle Guard: Real-time BFS reachability checks ensure biolog-
ical DAG acyclicity, preventing circular lineages.
• Cryogenic Grid Matrix: A responsive 10 × 10 freezer rack interface with auto-
matic coordinate indexing and collision prevention.
• Interactive React Flow Canvas: Visual exploration of cellular lineage trees
with color-coded node states, passage indicators, and direct detail drawers.
• Tamper-Proof Audit Logging: Immutable, append-only audit trail capturing
every system event with RFC 4180 CSV export capability.

4.1
SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
StemTrack employs a modular, four-tier enterprise architecture consisting of the Pre-
sentation Layer, Security Gateway Layer, Biological Governance & Workflow Layer,
and MongoDB Persistence Layer, as illustrated in Figure 1.
Figure 1: Four-Tier Enterprise System Architecture of StemTrack Platform
The four synchronized operational layers include:
1. Presentation Tier (Client-Side): Built with React 18 and Vite. Implements
single-page application (SPA) routing, interactive dashboard KPI cards, React
Flow lineage DAG visualizer, material workflow modals (Sample, Passage, Split,
Cryopreserve, Retrieve), cryogenic rack matrix, and RFC 4180 audit trail exporter.
10


=== PAGE 20 ===
2. Security Gateway Tier: Manages incoming HTTP/REST requests. Enforces
Helmet security headers, IP rate limiting (50 req/15min on auth routes), JWT
Bearer token authentication, and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) separating
Admin, Researcher, and Donor permissions.
3. Biological Governance & Workflow Tier: Contains the core domain guardrails:
the DAG Cycle Guard (BFS reachability engine), Compliance Gate (append-only
consent and active IRB verification), and Mongoose Multi-Document Transaction
Manager.
4. Data Persistence Tier (MongoDB Atlas): Houses isolated collections (Users,
EthicalApprovals, Donors, BiologicalMaterials, LineageEdges, Experiments,
AuditLogs) optimized with compound indexes and transactional ACID sessions.
4.2
MODULE DESCRIPTION
4.2.1
Authentication and RBAC Module
Controls user authentication using 24-hour JSON Web Tokens. Manages three access
roles: admin (IRB approvals, audit logs, system configuration), researcher (donor
intake, culture passaging, splitting, cryopreservation, assays), and donor (viewing
personal tissue records and consent history).
4.2.2
Ethical Approval and Consent Management Module
Tracks IRB approval protocols with protocol IDs, approval dates, expiration dates,
and operational statuses (APPROVED, EXPIRED, REVOKED). Maintains an append-only
ledger of donor consent events, tracking consent status (GIVEN, WITHDRAWN), legal
guardian signatures, and timestamps.
4.2.3
Biological Material Lifecycle Module
Manages biological entities through standardized lifecycle states (ACTIVE, CRYOPRESERVED,
UTILIZED, DISCARDED). Automates passage number incrementation (P0 →P1), via-
bility scoring, morphological categorization, and cell count tracking.
4.2.4
Lineage Graph and Cycle Prevention Module
Constructs directed edges connecting parent and child biological specimens. Main-
tains relationship types (DERIVED, PASSAGED, SPLIT, THAWED). Invokes the BFS Cycle
Guard before edge creation to prevent circular references.

The dynamic interaction sequence between actors, security middleware, domain guardrails,

in Figure 3.
The chronological execution sequence proceeds as follows:
1. Passage Request: The researcher submits a passage derivation request specify-
ing parent material ID and new culture parameters.
2. Security Interception: Express middleware validates the Bearer JWT token
and confirms the researcher role.
3. Consent Gate Check: The Compliance Gate queries the parent donor’s consent
history and IRB protocol status. If consent is WITHDRAWN or IRB is expired, the
request is immediately rejected (403 Forbidden).
4. BFS Cycle Guard Check: The graph engine runs BFS reachability from the
proposed child to the parent. If a path already exists, a cycle error is thrown
(400 Bad Request).
5. ACID Session Start: A MongoDB multi-document transaction session is initi-
ated.
6. Material Creation: The new passage culture (P + 1) is instantiated with status
ACTIVE.

CHAPTER 6
CONCLUSION & FUTURE WORK
6.1
CONCLUSION
The StemTrack platform delivers a comprehensive biological lineage tracking and
ethical governance solution tailored specifically to human stem cell research. By re-
placing static database updates with an event-sourced Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG),
the platform resolves key limitations of traditional Laboratory Information Manage-
ment Systems (LIMS).
Key achievements of this project include:
• Biological Provenance: Derivations, splits, passages, and cryopreservations are
modeled as immutable directed edges, establishing complete cellular genealogies.
• Mathematical Integrity: The Breadth-First Search DAG cycle prevention en-
gine guarantees that biological lineages remain strictly acyclic, rejecting circular
dependencies.
• Integrated Ethical Governance: Linking derivations directly to active IRB
protocols and append-only donor consent records ensures that consent revocations
propagate automatically across downstream experiments.
• ACID Reliability: MongoDB multi-document transactions guarantee that parent-
child state changes, edges, and audit records update atomically.
• Intuitive Visualization: An interactive React Flow canvas paired with a 10×10
cryogenic slot matrix provides researchers with clear insight into both cellular
lineages and physical freezer capacity.
